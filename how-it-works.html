How a clip is made

What clipfarm does, in the order it does it.

  1. Permission comes first

    A clip starts from one of three places: a streamer who told us yes, a streamer whose clipping program invites clips, or our own gameplay. Each yes is saved with the message it came in. No permission on record means the clip cannot be posted.

  2. Finding the moment

    clipfarm looks through that streamer's public clips and streams for moments worth sharing: a loud reaction, a busy chat, a joke that lands.

  3. Editing

    The moment is cut to under a minute and turned upright for phones. Software writes out what was said so the clip gets subtitles. The streamer's name goes on screen.

  4. Captions

    An AI writing tool drafts a few caption and title options. We pick one or write our own.

  5. A person approves it

    The channel's owner watches every clip. Nothing is uploaded until they press approve, and a clip they reject is never posted.

  6. Upload

    On TikTok the clip goes to our own account's inbox as a draft, and we post it by hand in the TikTok app. On YouTube, Instagram and Facebook clipfarm uploads it to our own channel, account and Page. Each post credits the streamer and links their channel.

What clipfarm uses TikTok for

clipfarm signs in to one TikTok account, the channel's own. It asks TikTok for two things: the account's basic profile, to confirm which account is signed in, and permission to send a video to that account's inbox as a draft. It does not read messages, followers, comments or watch history. The privacy policy lists every permission on every service.

Where it runs

clipfarm runs on the owner's own computer. Sign-in tokens stay in a locked folder there and go only to the service they belong to.

Privacy policy

clipfarm. Last updated 2026-11-20.

The short version

clipfarm is used by one person, the owner of the mAgNiFiCeNt_cLiPs channel, with their own TikTok, Google and Instagram accounts. Nobody else can sign in to it, and it collects no data about viewers.

What clipfarm gets from TikTok

When the owner signs in with TikTok, clipfarm asks for two permissions:

  • user.info.basic: the account's basic profile (its ID, name and picture), to confirm which account is signed in.
  • video.upload: to send a video to that account's inbox as a draft.

TikTok then gives clipfarm a login token for that one account.

What clipfarm gets from Google

When the owner signs in with Google, clipfarm asks for these permissions:

  • YouTube (youtube.upload, youtube.force-ssl): to upload the owner's finished clips to the owner's own channel and to read that channel's own video list and view counts.
  • Gmail (gmail.send, gmail.readonly): to send the emails the owner approved, one by one, from the channel's own mailbox to streamers, asking permission to clip them, and to look in that mailbox for their replies.
  • Google Drive (drive.file): to put a streamer's own finished clips in a folder for that streamer, when the streamer asked for them. clipfarm can see only the folders and files it made itself, nothing else in the Drive. Each folder holds one streamer's clips, and copies are deleted after 7 days.

Google then gives clipfarm a login token for each. A streamer's reply (their yes or no, and the address it came from) is saved on the owner's computer as the record of their answer. A streamer who asks not to be contacted again is put on a do-not-contact list and gets no more email.

clipfarm uses what it gets from Google only for the things listed above. It does not pass it to anyone else, does not use it for ads, and does not use it to train AI models.

What clipfarm gets from Instagram (Meta)

When the owner signs in with Facebook, clipfarm asks for permission to see which Facebook Page and Instagram account the owner manages (their names and IDs) and to publish videos to that Instagram account and that Page. Meta then gives clipfarm a login token for that one Page. clipfarm uses it only to post the owner's own finished clips as Reels, to delete one of them again when a streamer asks, and to read how many views, likes and comments those Reels have and how many followers the account and the Page have. It does not read messages, who the followers are, or what the comments say.

Where it is kept

The login tokens are stored in files in a locked folder on the owner's own computer. They are sent only to the service they belong to (TikTok, Google or Meta), and they are not shared with or sold to anyone.

What clipfarm does not do

  • It does not read the TikTok account's messages, followers, comments or watch history.
  • It does not delete or change mail, and it does not read YouTube viewers' personal data.
  • It does not collect data about viewers or about other TikTok users.
  • It shows no ads and tracks nobody.

Removing access

The owner can disconnect clipfarm at any time in the TikTok app's settings, in the list of apps that have access to the account, and for Google at myaccount.google.com/connections, and for Instagram in Facebook's settings under Business integrations. Deleting the token file on the computer removes the stored login. A streamer can ask for their saved reply to be deleted the same way as a clip: see below.

This website

These pages set no cookies and run no scripts. They are hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses as described in its own privacy statement.

Streamers in the clips

Clips show a streamer's public channel name as a credit. If you want a clip or your name removed, message @magnificent_clips0 on TikTok or @magnificent_clips on YouTube.
